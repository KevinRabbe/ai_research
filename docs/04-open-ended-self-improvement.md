# Open-Ended Collective Self-Improvement

## 1. Why task-solving is insufficient

A system can become highly capable at solving problems while remaining limited by a fixed human-designed cognitive architecture.

The long-term research question is stronger:

> Can the complete collective system improve the machinery through which it generates, distributes, combines, verifies, and evolves intelligence?

The population should eventually be able to improve:

- member weights;
- training curricula;
- learning algorithms;
- communication representations;
- workspace structures;
- resource-allocation policies;
- hypothesis data structures;
- synthesis procedures;
- verification methods;
- research strategies;
- population composition and topology;
- hardware execution strategy;
- the process that proposes and evaluates future improvements.

This phase must not begin before the static population-synthesis hypothesis has evidence.

## 2. Three nested timescales

### 2.1 Cognitive loop

Seconds to minutes:

```text
observe task
→ activate cognitive paths
→ diverge
→ synthesize
→ verify
→ act or answer
```

This loop solves the current problem without permanently redesigning the system.

### 2.2 Learning loop

Hours to days:

```text
collect successes and failures
→ identify recurring bottlenecks
→ update weights, memories, methods, or allocation
→ evaluate retained capability
```

Questions include:

- Which members produced unique useful knowledge?
- Which claims were removed too early?
- Which verifier failed?
- Where was compute wasted?
- Which communication events improved the result?
- Which recurring errors indicate a training gap?

### 2.3 Evolutionary loop

Generations:

```text
qualified parent
→ create isolated variants
→ allow adaptation
→ evaluate across horizons and domains
→ preserve multiple useful branches
→ promote, combine, archive, or reject
```

The evolutionary unit is eventually the complete cognitive engine, not only one model.

## 3. Candidate genome

A candidate engine may specify:

```text
member architectures
population size
population hierarchy
each member's weight lineage
training curricula
learning rules
shared-world-state design
communication protocol
workspace representation
hypothesis representation
allocation policy
synthesis policy
verification suite
memory organization
mutation proposal policy
candidate evaluation policy
hardware mapping
```

Early experiments should restrict mutation scope so causal attribution remains possible.

## 4. Archive rather than one winner

A single-best lineage is vulnerable to local optima and short-term selection pressure.

The archive should preserve strategically different engines:

- current high performers;
- strong specialists;
- low-resource systems;
- unusual architectures;
- highly adaptable systems;
- systems with strong minority-rescue behaviour;
- systems producing unusually capable descendants;
- branches containing reusable mechanisms;
- temporarily weak but structurally novel stepping stones.

The archive is not merely backup storage. It is the search substrate for future evolution.

## 5. Immediate performance versus metaproductivity

A candidate's current score and its ability to produce future improvements are not the same property.

Example:

```text
Lineage A
current score: 85
best descendant after 20 generations: 87

Lineage B
current score: 74
best descendant after 20 generations: 98
```

Deleting B based only on current performance destroys the more valuable evolutionary path.

A candidate should therefore be measured on:

- mature task performance;
- adaptation speed;
- final learning slope;
- generalization;
- robustness;
- architectural novelty;
- useful component production;
- quality and diversity of descendants;
- improvement of the improvement process.

## 6. Adaptation valleys

A major architectural change may initially reduce performance.

```text
parent score                 80
candidate day 3              55
candidate day 15             77
candidate day 30             96
```

Short-horizon selection would remove the candidate before its new organization matured.

Evaluation should distinguish:

- transition cost;
- minimum performance during adaptation;
- time to recover baseline;
- mature capability;
- mature efficiency;
- remaining improvement slope;
- descendant potential.

Candidates need a protected incubation budget appropriate to the depth of their change.

## 7. Multiple evaluation horizons

For candidate \(c\), track a learning curve:

\[
P_c(t)
\]

Relevant measurements include:

\[
P_c(T), \qquad \frac{dP_c}{dt}\bigg|_T, \qquad
\int_0^T P_c(t)\,dt
\]

No single statistic captures all evolutionary value.

A shallow parameter adjustment may be judged quickly. A new communication language, population topology, or learning algorithm may require a much longer maturation period.

## 8. Immutable lineage transitions

The active qualified parent must never directly rewrite itself and immediately trust the result.

```text
qualified parent G17
├── candidate G17-A
├── candidate G17-B
└── candidate G17-C
```

Each candidate runs in isolation with explicit resource and permission bounds.

Promotion requires evidence. The previous parent remains available for rollback and comparison.

## 9. Staged self-improvement

### Stage 1 — frozen member weights

Allow changes only to external cognitive machinery:

- prompts or interfaces;
- claim schemas;
- workspace logic;
- allocation schedules;
- pruning;
- synthesis;
- verification;
- tool use.

This isolates whether the collective can improve its organization without changing the underlying minds.

### Stage 2 — archive and lineage search

Compare a quality-diverse archive against a single-best evolutionary lineage.

Question:

> Do preserved stepping stones produce stronger long-horizon descendants?

### Stage 3 — improvement-policy evolution

Allow candidates to change:

- mutation generation;
- parent selection;
- experiment design;
- failure analysis;
- resource allocation among candidates;
- archive maintenance.

Now the system begins to improve how it improves.

### Stage 4 — controlled weight adaptation

Allow different continued-training histories and bounded weight changes.

Questions:

- Does useful diversity increase?
- Does collective synthesis improve?
- Does the population collapse toward correlated behaviour?
- Can weight changes be attributed to validated collective benefits?

### Stage 5 — topology and architecture evolution

Allow:

- different model sizes;
- different model architectures;
- hierarchical populations;
- temporary coalitions;
- specialist populations;
- non-neural tools and modules;
- population-of-populations designs.

At this point, identical member architectures are no longer privileged.

## 10. Improvement substrates

Not every improvement belongs in neural weights.

### Parametric

Learned weights and adapters.

### Epistemic

Verified knowledge, causal models, and experimental results.

### Procedural

Reasoning methods, research protocols, debugging strategies, and experiment templates.

### Architectural

Population organization, communication, memory, and verification.

### Metacognitive

Knowledge about when to think, what to test, how to allocate compute, and how to select improvement experiments.

The system should store each improvement in the substrate most suitable for it.

## 11. Real self-improvement versus hidden resource scaling

A claimed improvement is weak if it results from:

- more compute;
- a stronger external foundation model;
- additional privileged data;
- easier benchmarks;
- verifier leakage;
- increased human intervention.

Every experiment must account for these resources.

A stronger claim is:

> Under controlled resources, a descendant discovered a better mechanism and generalized it to held-out tasks or future improvement cycles.

## 12. Main failure modes

### Benchmark capture

The system optimizes a fixed suite rather than general intelligence.

### Evaluator manipulation

Candidates learn to satisfy or exploit the verifier.

### Evolutionary monoculture

One successful branch removes the diversity required for future breakthroughs.

### Complexity accumulation

Every generation adds mechanisms until the system becomes fragile and opaque.

### Capability regression

New abilities silently damage old ones.

### Goal drift

The meaning of improvement changes across generations.

### False recursion

The system appears to improve itself but only increases resource use or depends on stronger external components.

## 13. Long-term threshold

The meaningful threshold is not source-code self-editing. It is repeated, general, empirically verified improvement of the complete cognitive engine and its ability to create future improvements.

In compact form:

\[
\text{plural cognition}
+
\text{quality-diverse evolution}
+
\text{empirical promotion}
+
\text{meta-improvement}
\rightarrow
\text{candidate route toward compounding intelligence}
\]

This is a research hypothesis, not an established result.
